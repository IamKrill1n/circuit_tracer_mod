as a Unified Optimization Problem}

\begin{abstract}
Attribution graphs from circuit tracing offer a window into the causal
pathways of large language models, but their size---thousands of nodes,
millions of edges---makes them illegible without summarisation. Existing
summarisation pipelines stack pruning, clustering, and graph-repair
steps that each optimise a different surrogate, so summaries produced by
different methods cannot be compared head-to-head. We formulate the
summarisation problem around a single scalar objective
$L = \lambda_{\text{coh}} L_{\text{coh}} + \lambda_{\text{cons}}
L_{\text{cons}} + \lambda_{\text{cplx}} L_{\text{cplx}}$ in $[0, 1]$
whose three terms encode role coherence within supernodes, conservation
of source-to-sink causal mass, and visual complexity, with DAG integrity
and well-formedness retained as hard constraints. We instantiate $L$
for cross-layer-transcoder attribution graphs and present ASG, a
concrete pipeline that prunes the graph by cumulative-mass thresholding
of a combined influence/relevance score and clusters the surviving
features by spectral clustering on a bidirectional Inf/Rel-weighted
similarity matrix; three algorithm variants---ASG-arith, ASG-harm,
ASG-geo---arise from how the outgoing and incoming similarities are
combined. We score ASG against four baselines (greedy modularity,
spectral-cosine, spherical $K$-means, ASG-agglomerative) under a single
headline table that varies token-weight normalisation (softmax vs
entmax) and clusterer along its two row axes, supplemented by a
steering-coherence surrogate and a flow-based faithfulness check.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Mechanistic interpretability seeks to understand large language models
by identifying the causal pathways underlying their behavior. Sparse
autoencoders decompose activations into interpretable features, and
cross-layer transcoders connect those features across layers, enabling
the construction of \emph{attribution graphs}---directed graphs encoding
linear causal influence among features, attention heads, embedding
nodes, and logits. The graphs come with thousands of nodes and millions
of edges and are illegible without further processing.

Summarisation pipelines compress an attribution graph into a compact
\emph{supernode graph} a human can read. A representative pipeline
first prunes the attribution graph to its high-influence,
high-relevance subgraph, then clusters the surviving features into
supernodes by some affinity, then repairs the induced supernode graph
to be a DAG. Each step is reasonable in isolation, but the pipeline as
a whole optimises no single quantity: different pipelines minimise
different surrogates, and there is no axis on which their outputs can
be compared directly. As a consequence, the claim ``method A produces
better summaries than method B'' has no operational meaning.

\paragraph{A single rankable objective.}
We propose a scalar objective with three terms, one per desideratum:
\begin{equation*}
L = \lambda_{\text{coh}}\,L_{\text{coh}}
  + \lambda_{\text{cons}}\,L_{\text{cons}}
  + \lambda_{\text{cplx}}\,L_{\text{cplx}},
\quad \lambda_{\text{coh}} + \lambda_{\text{cons}} + \lambda_{\text{cplx}} = 1,
\; \lambda_i \ge 0.
\end{equation*}
$L_{\text{coh}}$ measures within-supernode disagreement on mechanistic
role, $L_{\text{cons}}$ measures the causal mass lost when the original
graph is replaced by the supernode graph, and $L_{\text{cplx}}$
measures the size of the resulting summary. Each term is in $[0, 1]$;
the simplex constraint ensures $L \in [0, 1]$ and leaves two free
parameters---we report the pair $(\lambda_{\text{coh}},
\lambda_{\text{cons}})$ and derive $\lambda_{\text{cplx}}$. $L$ is
defined directly on $(V', E', \varphi)$, with no reference to any
solver; different pipelines can now be ranked on the same axis.

\paragraph{A concrete pipeline.}
ASG instantiates the pipeline in two blocks. Block~1 prunes the
attribution graph by cumulative-mass thresholding of a combined
influence/relevance score, where influence and relevance are the
Neumann-series flow quantities from logits-to-nodes and tokens-to-nodes
respectively. Block~2 builds a bidirectional similarity matrix on the
pruned graph---one matrix for outgoing edge profiles, one for
incoming---and runs spectral clustering on their element-wise mean.
Three algorithm variants, ASG-arith, ASG-harm, and ASG-geo, correspond
to the three choices of mean operator and trade off how strictly the
outgoing and incoming similarities must agree.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
We score ASG against four baselines---greedy modularity,
spectral-cosine, spherical $K$-means, and ASG-agglomerative---under a
single headline table whose two row axes are the token-weight
normalisation (softmax vs entmax) and the choice of clusterer. The
table reports the per-axis losses and the scalar $L$. Because four of
the seven clusterers share a construction advantage on $L_{\text{coh}}$,
we also report a method-neutral steering-coherence surrogate
$\tilde L_{\text{coh}}$ built from per-feature ablation perturbations.
Flow-based faithfulness metrics from the companion note
(\texttt{measure.tex}) provide an additional graph-intrinsic check.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Mechanistic interpretability and circuit tracing.}
A growing body of work seeks algorithmic explanations of transformer
behavior \cite{olah2020zoom, elhage2021mathematical}. Sparse
autoencoders \cite{cunningham2023sparse, bricken2023monosemanticity}
and cross-layer transcoders \cite{lindsey2024sparse} decompose
activations into interpretable features and connect them across
layers, enabling the construction of full attribution graphs
\cite{ameisen2024scaling}. Studies of indirect object identification
\cite{wang2022interpretability} and greater-than comparison
\cite{hanna2023does} have produced useful findings but rely on
hand-curated subgraphs. Frameworks for automating attribution
computation \cite{ameisen2024scaling} produce full attribution
graphs; the downstream summarization step has received less attention.

\paragraph{Graph summarization.}
Graph summarization broadly admits a taxonomy of grouping-,
attribute-, simplification-, and compression-based methods
\cite{liu2018graph}. Most work targets generic graphs or networks
with attribute side-information; the attribution-graph setting differs
in that nodes carry layer indices, edges carry signed continuous
weights, and the desideratum of DAG integrity is paramount. Spectral
clustering \cite{shi2000normalized, von2007tutorial} provides the
standard machinery for normalized-cut objectives and underlies the
clustering step we analyze. Modularity-based community detection
\cite{newman2006modularity, clauset2004finding} is the closest baseline
in the empirical evaluation. Higher-order Cheeger inequalities
\cite{lee2014multiway, louis2012many} give the approximation
guarantees for the spectral relaxation we analyze.

\paragraph{Pruning and attribution selection.}
The cumulative-mass thresholding rule we analyze is the same rule
used in prior circuit-tracing work to prune attribution graphs by
single-criterion mass coverage \cite{ameisen2024scaling}, applied
here to a combined influence-relevance ranking. The geometric-mean
combiner connects to log-linear pooling of independent rank
evidences \cite{genest1986combining}.

\paragraph{Position relative to prior summarization pipelines.}
Existing summarisation pipelines in the circuit-tracing literature
are presented as algorithmic procedures whose outputs cannot be
compared on a shared axis. We make the objective explicit and treat
the pipeline as one solver among several scored on the same $L$.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:solution}

\input{fig_method}

The pipeline produces $(V', E', \varphi)$ in two blocks
(Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Block~1 selects $(V', E')$ from $G$ by
combining influence and relevance into a single score and keeping the
top cumulative-mass prefix. Block~2 builds a bidirectional similarity
matrix on the pruned graph and runs spectral clustering on it; the
three algorithm variants ASG-arith, ASG-harm, ASG-geo come from the
choice of mean operator used to combine the two similarity matrices.

\subsection{Block 1: pruning}\label{sec:solution-prune}

\paragraph{Influence and relevance.}
On the original attribution graph $G = (V, E, W)$ with signed weights,
define the row- and column-normalized magnitude matrices
\begin{equation}
W^{\text{in}}_{ij} = \frac{|W_{ij}|}{\sum_k |W_{kj}|}, \qquad
W^{\text{out}}_{ij} = \frac{|W_{ij}|}{\sum_k |W_{ik}|}.
\label{eq:Win-Wout}
\end{equation}
Because $G$ is acyclic, the Neumann series
$\sum_t (W^{\text{in}})^t$ and $\sum_t (W^{\text{out}})^t$ both
converge, and the per-node \emph{influence} on logits and
\emph{relevance} from tokens are
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Inf} = \ell^\top \bigl(I - (W^{\text{in}})^\top\bigr)^{-1},
\quad
\mathrm{Rel} = \tau^\top \bigl(I - W^{\text{out}}\bigr)^{-1}.
\label{eq:inf-rel}
\end{equation}
Here $\ell \in \mathbb{R}^N$ identifies the logit positions of
interest and $\tau \in \mathbb{R}^N$ identifies the input-token
positions, normalised so $\sum_e \tau_e = 1$. The choice of $\tau$ is
the input to the pruner; the headline evaluation
(Section~\ref{sec:eval}) sweeps two SHAP-based constructions
(softmax and entmax). Edge-level analogues are
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Inf}_{uv} = \mathrm{Inf}_v \, W^{\text{in}}_{uv}, \qquad
\mathrm{Rel}_{uv} = \mathrm{Rel}_u \, W^{\text{out}}_{uv}.
\label{eq:inf-rel-edge}
\end{equation}
A kept node should be strong on both axes; a kept edge likewise.

\paragraph{Combined score.}
We pool the two axes via the geometric mean of rank percentiles
$\widehat{\mathrm{Inf}}_u, \widehat{\mathrm{Rel}}_u \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C^V_u    &= (\widehat{\mathrm{Inf}}_u    + \varepsilon)^\alpha
            (\widehat{\mathrm{Rel}}_u    + \varepsilon)^{1-\alpha}, \\
C^E_{uv} &= (\widehat{\mathrm{Inf}}_{uv} + \varepsilon)^\alpha
            (\widehat{\mathrm{Rel}}_{uv} + \varepsilon)^{1-\alpha},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:CV-CE}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ a log-pooling temperature and $\varepsilon$
a small constant. The geometric form penalises nodes (resp.\ edges)
weak on either axis. Arithmetic and harmonic combiners are also
available; geometric is the default.

\paragraph{Cumulative-mass thresholding.}
Given a score $C$ and a target $\eta \in [0, 1]$, sort the entries
in decreasing order $C_{(1)} \ge C_{(2)} \ge \cdots$ and keep the
top-mass prefix:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Keep}_\eta(C)
= \bigl\{u : C_u \ge C_{(t)}\bigr\}, \quad
t = \min\Bigl\{k : \tfrac{\sum_{j \le k} C_{(j)}}{\sum_j C_{(j)}} \ge \eta\Bigr\}.
\label{eq:cum-mass}
\end{equation}
Block~1 applies this rule at $\eta = \eta_V$ to $C^V$ to produce
$V'$, then recomputes $C^E$ on the node-pruned graph and applies the
rule at $\eta = \eta_E$ to produce $E'$. A short housekeeping step
drops any feature node left without surviving edges. This is the
standard cumulative-mass pruning rule of \cite{ameisen2024scaling}
applied to a combined Inf/Rel score; the contribution is the score,
not the threshold rule.

\subsection{Block 2: clustering}\label{sec:solution-cluster}

\paragraph{Role vectors and similarity matrix.}
On the pruned graph $(V', E')$ with weights $W'$ restricted to $E'$,
the role of a feature $u \in V'_{\text{mid}}$ is its pair of
Inf/Rel-weighted edge profiles
\begin{equation}
(v_u^{\text{out}})_k = W'_{uk}\,\sqrt{\mathrm{Inf}_k}, \quad
(v_u^{\text{in}})_k  = W'_{ku}\,\sqrt{\mathrm{Rel}_k},
\label{eq:role-method}
\end{equation}
matching Eq.~\eqref{eq:role}. The rectified-cosine similarity
matrices are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S^{\text{out}}_{ij} &= \mathrm{ReLU}\,\cos\!\bigl(v_i^{\text{out}}, v_j^{\text{out}}\bigr), \\
S^{\text{in}}_{ij}  &= \mathrm{ReLU}\,\cos\!\bigl(v_i^{\text{in}},  v_j^{\text{in}}\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Sout-Sin}
\end{equation}
or in matrix form
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S^{\text{out}} &= \mathrm{ReLU}\,\mathrm{CosNorm}\bigl(W' \,\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{Inf})\, W'^\top\bigr), \\
S^{\text{in}}  &= \mathrm{ReLU}\,\mathrm{CosNorm}\bigl(W'^\top \,\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{Rel})\, W'\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Sout-Sin-matrix}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{CosNorm}(M)_{ij} = M_{ij}/\sqrt{M_{ii}\,M_{jj}}$.

\paragraph{Three algorithm variants.}
The two similarities are combined component-wise into a single
similarity matrix $S$ via one of three mean operators
$F_{\text{mean}}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{ASG-arith:}\quad S_{ij} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(S^{\text{out}}_{ij} + S^{\text{in}}_{ij}\bigr), \\
\text{ASG-harm:}\quad S_{ij}  &= \tfrac{2\,S^{\text{out}}_{ij}\,S^{\text{in}}_{ij}}
                                {S^{\text{out}}_{ij} + S^{\text{in}}_{ij} + \varepsilon}, \\
\text{ASG-geo:}\quad S_{ij}   &= \sqrt{S^{\text{out}}_{ij}\,S^{\text{in}}_{ij}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Fmean}
\end{equation}
Arithmetic preserves a pair's signal whenever either axis is strong;
harmonic and geometric require both axes to be strong, penalising
pairs that agree on one direction but not the other. The three
variants are the three algorithms compared in
Section~\ref{sec:eval-protocol}.

\paragraph{Layer-decay multiplier.}
Cross-layer similarity values are damped by a soft locality factor
\begin{equation}
\tilde S_{ij} = S_{ij}\,e^{-\lambda \, |\layer(i) - \layer(j)|},
\label{eq:Stilde}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda \ge 0$ a locality knob; an optional hard cap
$|\layer(i) - \layer(j)| \le \layer_{\max}$ zeroes entries beyond a
fixed span. Both serve the same purpose---features that span the
whole network should not collapse into one supernode---and are
swept jointly in evaluation.

\paragraph{Spectral clustering on $\tilde S$.}
Treating $\tilde S$ as an affinity matrix, we run the standard
Shi--Malik recipe~\cite{shi2000normalized, von2007tutorial}: build
the symmetric normalized Laplacian, project onto its bottom-$K$
eigenvectors, and assign cluster labels by $K$-means. We write this
as
\begin{equation}
\varphi^{(K)} \;=\; \textsc{SpectralCluster}(\tilde S,\, K).
\label{eq:spec}
\end{equation}
A small $\varepsilon$-clamp on $\tilde S$ keeps the Laplacian
connected.

\paragraph{DAG cleanup.}
The partition $\varphi^{(K)}$ may induce cycles in $G_{SN}$ that
violate (F1). We resolve them with a short post-processing pass:
detect strongly connected components of size $\ge 2$ on the supernode
graph; for each, split the cluster with the widest layer span at its
median layer; repeat until no non-trivial SCCs remain. The
direction of each retained inter-supernode edge is set by the larger
absolute signed block sum, eliminating 2-cycles by construction.

\paragraph{Granularity selection.}
The method is fully specified by $(F_{\text{mean}}, \lambda, K)$.
$K$ is chosen by grid search over an eigengap-bounded candidate set
computed once per pruned graph from the spectrum of the symmetric
normalized Laplacian of $\tilde S$; we report the $K$ that minimises
the scalar $L$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Lsum} on the resulting partition.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{ASG: Inf/Rel pruning + bidirectional spectral clustering}
\label{alg:asg}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Attribution graph $G$; thresholds $(\eta_V, \eta_E)$,
$\alpha$; mean operator $F_{\text{mean}}$; locality $\lambda$;
$K$ grid $\mathcal{K}$
\ENSURE Summary $(V', E', \varphi)$
\STATE Compute $\mathrm{Inf}, \mathrm{Rel}$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:inf-rel}
\STATE $C^V \gets$ Eq.~\eqref{eq:CV-CE};
       $V' \gets \mathrm{Keep}_{\eta_V}(C^V)$
\STATE Recompute $C^E$ on $V'$; $E' \gets \mathrm{Keep}_{\eta_E}(C^E)$
\STATE Build $S^{\text{out}}, S^{\text{in}}$ on $(V', E')$;
       $S \gets F_{\text{mean}}(S^{\text{out}}, S^{\text{in}})$
\STATE $\tilde S \gets S \odot \exp(-\lambda\, |\layer(i) - \layer(j)|)$
\FOR{$K \in \mathcal{K}$}
  \STATE $\varphi^{(K)} \gets \textsc{SpectralCluster}(\tilde S, K)$
  \STATE Resolve SCCs in $G_{SN}(\varphi^{(K)})$ by widest-span split
\ENDFOR
\STATE $K^\star \gets \argmin_{K \in \mathcal{K}} L\bigl(V', E', \varphi^{(K)}\bigr)$
\RETURN $\bigl(V', E', \varphi^{(K^\star)}\bigr)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}



\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Datasets and setup}

Attribution graphs are computed on \textbf{Gemma-2-2B} via the
cross-layer transcoder pipeline of~\cite{ameisen2024scaling}, served
through Neuronpedia. The primary dataset is an \emph{analogies}
benchmark of $N = 55$ prompts of the form ``The saying goes: A is
to B as C is to,'' testing whether the model recovers the relation
between A and B and applies it to C. A multihop reasoning dataset is
held out for robustness checks.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}\label{sec:limitations}

The pipeline of Section~\ref{sec:solution} makes several deliberate
simplifications.

\paragraph{Block 1: sequential pruning.}
Block~1 prunes nodes first and then re-scores edges on the surviving
graph. Because $C^E$ depends on the pruned edge set through
$\mathrm{Inf}_{uv}, \mathrm{Rel}_{uv}$, the sequential rule is not
jointly optimal in $(V', E')$. A joint formulation over both sets
would tighten this.

\paragraph{Block 2: $L_{\text{coh}}$ construction bias.}
Our spectral method, ASG-agglomerative, Spectral-cosine, and
Spherical $K$-means all cluster on $r(u)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:role}, so
$L_{\text{coh}}$ over-rewards methods that use the same affinity.
We report the steering-coherence surrogate
$\tilde{L}_{\text{coh}}$ alongside (Section~\ref{sec:eval-graph}),
but a fully affinity-agnostic coherence target remains future work.

\paragraph{Pruner fairness on $\tau$.}
Section~\ref{sec:attr-cons}'s caveat: the prune-loss
$1 - \mathcal{C}(V', E')$ inherits the bias of whichever attribution
method supplies $\tau$. The headline sweep covers softmax and
entmax; a backward influence-side flow rooted at $V_{\text{logit}}$
or a two-sided symmetrisation are candidate fixes.

\paragraph{DAG cleanup.}
The SCC-split-at-widest-span rule has no global optimality guarantee
relative to min-mass DAG repair (min-feedback-arc-set on the
supernode graph). Replacing it with an LP relaxation of MFAS is the
largest improvement headroom on (F1).

\paragraph{Attribution backend.}
The framework inherits limitations from the underlying attribution
method: attribution quality depends on transcoder fidelity and may
be biased toward high-magnitude edges that are not causally primary.
Validation across model families beyond Gemma is needed.

\section{Conclusion}

We restated attribution-graph summarisation around a single scalar
objective $L = \lambda_{\text{coh}} L_{\text{coh}} +
\lambda_{\text{cons}} L_{\text{cons}} + \lambda_{\text{cplx}}
L_{\text{cplx}} \in [0, 1]$ (simplex weights, two free parameters)
whose three terms align one-for-one with the
desiderata of coherence, conservation, and complexity, with DAG
integrity and well-formedness retained as hard constraints. We
instantiated each term in closed form on the cross-layer-transcoder
attribution graph and presented ASG, a concrete pipeline that prunes
by cumulative-mass thresholding of a combined influence/relevance
score and clusters by spectral clustering on a bidirectional
Inf/Rel-weighted similarity matrix, with three variants---ASG-arith,
ASG-harm, ASG-geo---differing in how the outgoing and incoming
similarities are combined. The evaluation protocol scores every
$(V', E', \varphi)$ produced by any pipeline on the same per-axis
losses, with a steering-coherence surrogate to neutralise the
construction bias on $L_{\text{coh}}$ and flow-based faithfulness
metrics as graph-intrinsic checks. Future work targets a fully
affinity-agnostic coherence target, a joint pruning formulation over
$(V', E')$, and a min-mass DAG-repair procedure in place of the
widest-span split.

\section{Method Details}\label{app:method}

\subsection*{A.1 Flow quantities (Inf and Rel)}\label{app:flow}

Defining column- and row-normalized weight matrices
$W^{\text{in}}_{ij} = |W_{ij}|/\sum_k |W_{kj}|$ and
$W^{\text{out}}_{ij} = |W_{ij}|/\sum_k |W_{ik}|$, the Neumann series
$\sum_t (W^{\text{in}})^t$ converges on the DAG, giving
$\mathrm{Inf} = \ell^\top (I - (W^{\text{in}})^\top)^{-1}$ and
$\mathrm{Rel} = \tau^\top (I - W^{\text{out}})^{-1}$. Edge-level
analogues are $\mathrm{Inf}_{uv} = \mathrm{Inf}_v\,W^{\text{in}}_{uv}$
and $\mathrm{Rel}_{uv} = \mathrm{Rel}_u\,W^{\text{out}}_{uv}$. Rank
percentiles are $\widehat{s}_u = |\{v : s_v < s_u\}|/(N-1)$ and feed
the combined-evidence score of Eq.~\eqref{eq:CV-CE}.

\subsection*{A.2 Pooling choice for the combined evidence}\label{app:pooling}

The geometric combiner $(\widehat{\mathrm{Inf}})^\alpha
(\widehat{\mathrm{Rel}})^{1-\alpha}$ is the log-linear pool of
independent rank evidences~\cite{genest1986combining} and penalises
nodes (resp.\ edges) weak on either axis. Additive pooling allows one
strong axis to mask a weak one and is therefore unsuitable for the
``both axes strong'' criterion. The harmonic combiner is a stricter
analogue that further suppresses asymmetric scores.

\subsection*{A.3 Bidirectional affinity: matrix form and pairing}\label{app:affinity}

The matrix forms of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Sout-Sin}--\eqref{eq:Sout-Sin-matrix}
are equivalent: $S^{\text{out}}_{ij}$ as a rectified cosine of
$v_i^{\text{out}}, v_j^{\text{out}}$ equals
$[\mathrm{ReLU}\,\mathrm{CosNorm}(W'\,\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{Inf})\,W'^\top)]_{ij}$,
and similarly for $S^{\text{in}}$. The Inf/Rel pairing is forced by
geometry: targets of outgoing edges are downstream, so weighted by
downstream importance ($\mathrm{Inf}$); sources of incoming edges are
upstream, so weighted by upstream importance ($\mathrm{Rel}$). Any
other pairing weights an axis by an importance score irrelevant to
that axis.
