Validating the Pruning Threshold

From a subjective θ = 0.01 / 0.02 to a defensible, rule-derived choice.

Summary

The threshold feels arbitrary because we're defending the number. Stop. Defend the selection procedure — the same move Anthropic makes: they never claim 0.8 is optimal, they "chart a frontier between the number of {nodes, edges} and {replacement, completeness} scores" and read a point off it. A number that falls out of a stated rule is principled; a number we typed is not.

Plan: (1) state precisely what θ parameterizes, (2) sweep θ over the analogies dataset, (3) chart graph size vs. causal faithfulness, (4) select θ by a pre-registered rule (faithfulness floor, corroborated by the curve's knee), (5) show the choice is robust and beats baselines — all aggregated with error bars, not on one example.

1  What θ actually means be precise first

In find_threshold (summarization/graph_utils.py:75), the node mask is node_score ≥ find_threshold(node_score, θ), where the cutoff is the score at the cumulative-mass index:

k*= mink {k: ∑i=0ks(i) ∑iNsi ≥θ} ,   keep nodes with  si≥s(k*)

So θ is the cumulative fraction of (rank-normalized, combined influence×relevance) score mass retained by the top nodes — not a raw edge weight. Larger θ ⇒ keep more nodes.

  • Why 0.01–0.02 is reasonable here: because scores are rank-normalized (normalization="rank"), the mass is near-uniform across nodes, so a 1–2% cumulative cut still yields ~8–27 feature nodes — not one. This is not comparable to Anthropic's 0.8, which is cumulative influence mass.
  • Action: say this explicitly in the paper. Otherwise a reviewer who knows the Anthropic methods will expect θ ≈ 0.8 and assume a bug.
  • Already half-principled: θ is a fraction of retained relevance/influence, not an opaque cutoff. The only open question is which fraction — that's what §3–§6 settle.

2  The precedent we're matching

Anthropic (Attribution Graphs, Methods → Graph Pruning) justify their threshold pragmatically via a tradeoff curve, not an optimum:

  • Default θ = 0.8 → ~10× fewer nodes at the cost of only ~20% completeness.
  • Acronym example: θ = 0.8 → 55 nodes, completeness 0.70; θ = 0.95 → 236 nodes, completeness 0.87.
  • Their stated justification: "chart a frontier between the number of {nodes, edges} and {replacement, completeness} scores." No magic number is derived.

So the bar to clear is not "prove 0.01 is optimal." It's "show 0.01 sits at a sensible point on a size–faithfulness frontier, by a rule we fix in advance." We can clear that bar more rigorously than the original (aggregate + causal + baselines), which is itself a contribution.

3  The argument: four pillars

A. Size–faithfulness frontier + knee

Sweep θ; for each, plot a faithfulness metric (y) against graph size (x = # feature nodes or # edges). The curve is concave: show 0.01/0.02 lands at/just past the knee — beyond it, extra nodes buy negligible faithfulness. "Most of the recoverable behavior in the smallest graph" is the whole claim.

B. Causal faithfulness floor headline

Pre-register an acceptance rule and let θ be its consequence:

θ*= minθ {θ:  medianp∈D P(target | pruned) P(target | baseline) ≥τ}

e.g. τ = 0.8. Then the paper reads: "We retain the smallest circuit that preserves ≥ 80% of the model's target probability (median over the analogies set); this yields θ ≈ 0.01–0.02." The number is now derived. This is exactly what eval/eval_faithfulness.py computes — dropped CLT features zero-ablated, errors/embeddings/logits untouched, baseline read from the AttrGraph's token_prob.

C. Robustness band

Show downstream conclusions (clustering, the supernode story, steering deltas) are invariant across ~0.005–0.05. If the exact value doesn't change the findings, "arbitrary" loses its force — the result is a stable plateau, not a knife-edge.

D. Baseline domination

At equal node budget, our θ + scoring must beat (i) random node pruning and (ii) uniform / Anthropic-style influence-only pruning. Same x (size), higher y (faithfulness). This attributes the quality to the method, not just to keeping enough nodes. eval/analyze_prune.py already compares normalization schemes vs. baselines.

Aggregate everything. Each curve/number is a median (or mean ± IQR/SE) over the analogies dataset — never one prompt. A threshold defended across N prompts is the single biggest credibility lever.

6  What to write in the paper

Replace "we use θ = 0.01" with a rule + evidence paragraph:

"We do not tune the pruning threshold to a fixed value. Following the size–faithfulness tradeoff of [Anthropic], we sweep θ and select the smallest threshold whose pruned circuit preserves at least τ = 0.8 of the model's target probability under feature ablation, taken as the median over the analogies set. This rule yields θ ∈ [0.01, 0.02], which coincides with the knee of the node-count–vs–faithfulness frontier (Fig. X) and lies on a plateau where clustering and steering results are unchanged (Fig. Y). At matched node budgets our pruning dominates random and influence-only baselines (Fig. Z)."

That single paragraph converts the criticism into a strength: the threshold is a reproducible consequence of a pre-registered faithfulness target, triangulated three ways.

7  Reviewer objections, pre-answered

"Validated on one example." → Every figure is a median ± spread over the analogies dataset.
"Graph-internal faithfulness ≠ real faithfulness." → The headline curve is causal (eval_faithfulness.py, actual ablated forward passes); proxies only fill in the dense sweep and are shown to agree.
"Cherry-picked metric." → completeness, replacement, token-faithfulness, and causal P(target) all knee in the same region.
"Threshold tuned on the test set." → It's fixed by a rule (τ), and §C shows a flat plateau, so there is effectively nothing to tune; optionally select τ on a split and report on a held-out split.
"Why not just use Anthropic's 0.8?" → Different normalization (rank vs. influence-mass): clarify the semantics (§1) and show the frontier, where 0.8 over-retains for our score definition.

8  Showing the real strength: the interpretability axis read this

Faithfulness alone will not distinguish us from influence-only pruning — recovering P(target) is exactly what influence-only greedily maximizes. Our contribution is different: at equal size we discard uninteresting nodes (syntactic features, stop words, scaffolding) that influence-only keeps. So Pillar D must be scored on an interpretability axis, and the comparison is a faithfulness–interpretability Pareto, not a faithfulness race.

Circularity warning. relevance_conservation_rate and token_attribution_faithfulness (eval_prune.py) weight by the same SHAP token weights our pruning uses. Winning on them is self-fulfilling — a reviewer will reject it. Keep them as diagnostics; the win must use an interestingness label independent of the SHAP weights.

Ours sits up-and-to-the-right on interpretability for a small x cost. Guardrail: score feature-level metrics on CLT features only — for embedding nodes the stop-word down-weighting is circular by construction; the non-trivial claim is that content-token routing drops syntactic features.

Recommendation: lead with density Pareto (#1) + AUC (#3), steering (#4) as capstone; demote the SHAP-weighted faithfulness metrics to diagnostics.
