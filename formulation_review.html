Review: the intra + inter + complexity summarization loss

Attribution-graph summarization, "pruned graph as a weighted causal DAG" framing. Reviewing the proposed factoring L = Lintra + Linter + Lcplx, the two stated problems, and the steering behavior in replacement_model_transformerlens.py.

Verdict at a glance

  • Code check confirmed. Steering propagates through the real model to all downstream features that read the modified residual stream — independent of the attribution edges, and totally independent of which edges the summary kept or dropped. The graph is never consulted during intervention.
  • Problem 1 (missing pruning loss) is valid and is the important one. Pruning is the only summarization step that destroys behaviorally-real causal mass. Dropping it from the objective is backwards: you kept the artifact term and dropped the real one.
  • Problem 2 (inter loss is a visualization trick) is substantially correct, with one correction. The DAG-forcing loss is reconstruction-fidelity of the drawing, not of any causal quantity. It should be reclassified as a topological-violation penalty, not a conservation loss. The "effects cancel because they make cycles" intuition is really a clamp-override argument, not cancellation.
  • Bonus issue. Counting intra-cluster edge weight as a loss contradicts the causal-DAG merge rationale (strong mutual edges are what justify a merge). Make Lintra = coherence only.

1. The code finding, in detail

Trace of feature_intervention(..., constrained_layers=None) (the default used for steering):

  • calculate_delta_hook (L648): delta = (target value − current activation), mapped through the feature's decoder vector into layer_deltas; for a cross-layer transcoder it is spread across every downstream layer (L687–689).
  • intervention_hook (L691): adds layer_deltas[layer] to the residual stream at the feature-output hook, for all layers (intervention_range = range(n_layers), L710).
  • The forward pass continues; downstream transcoders re-encode the perturbed residual stream (calculate_delta_hook reads the live activation_cache, L654–656). Attention patterns are frozen by default; MLP/transcoder nonlinearities are not.

Consequence: a steered feature affects every downstream feature whose input reads the modified residual stream — whether or not an attribution edge connects them, and regardless of the summary's kept/dropped edges. The attribution graph (and its DAG-forced supernode view) is a linear approximation computed separately; the intervention does not use it. This is exactly why edges removed in the DAG-forcing step do not change steering outcomes.

2. The crux: two different ground truths

The confusion in both problems comes from conflating two things a "loss" could measure fidelity to:

(A) The linear graph G′(B) The model under intervention
What it is The pruned weighted DAG (V′, E′, W′), a static linear object. (I−W′)⁻¹ gives path sums. The real model's logit response to clamping/steering a supernode (eval_intervention.py).
What summarization does to it Clustering + DAG-forcing distort the graph as a drawing. Pruning removes nodes/edges. Clustering and DAG-forcing do nothing — the model is unchanged. Only pruning (which features you choose to talk about) matters, and even then only because you measure the effect of the kept set.
Cheap to compute?Yes, graph-intrinsic.No, needs forward passes.

The causal-DAG markdown (Frobenius reconstruction ‖B − BGH‖, ‖A − AGH‖) measures (A). Your intra/inter terms also measure (A). Problem 2 is the observation that for the inter term, (A) and (B) diverge so badly that the (A)-loss is meaningless as a stand-in for (B). The fix is not to delete the term but to stop selling it as a causal/behavioral loss.

3. Problem 1 — the missing pruning loss (valid, and it is the real one)

Your pruned node graph is already a DAG at the node level (topological order by layer/position). So at the node level there is no acyclicity loss and no clustering loss — those only appear once you collapse nodes into supernodes. The only step that changes what reaches the logits in the actual model is pruning: dropping a node deletes every forward path through it.

The flow-completeness term from the earlier version captured exactly this:

𝒞(V′, E′) = Σℓ ∈ Vlogit FlowV′(ℓ) ∈ [0,1],    prune loss = 1 − 𝒞

where Flow is τ injected at the sources and propagated by the row/column-normalized transition matrix on the DAG. This is the term most aligned with ground truth (B): the mass that no longer reaches a logit is mass your summary literally cannot explain. Dropping it while keeping the DAG-forcing term inverts the priority.

Two ways to put it back:

  • Explicit fourth term: L = Lintra + Linter + Lprune + Lcplx, with Lprune = 1 − 𝒞 depending only on (V′, E′) and τ.
  • Define all losses against the full graph G (not the pruned G′). Then pruned edges show up as "edges whose endpoints map to nothing," and the prune mass falls out of the same reconstruction sum that produces intra/inter. Cleaner, but couples the terms.

Recommendation: explicit fourth term. It keeps prune loss interpretable and partition-invariant, and it is the one number you can also validate behaviorally.

4. Problem 2 — the inter / DAG-forcing loss (a drawing artifact)

Where the supernode cycles even come from

The node graph is acyclic. A 2-cycle in the supernode graph (BST ≠ 0 and BTS ≠ 0) appears only because a supernode spans layers: S has low-layer members feeding high-layer members of T (the S→T mass) and T has low-layer members feeding high-layer members of S (the T→S mass). Both are genuine forward edges in G′. The dominant-direction rule keeps the bigger block and zeroes

dropped per pair = min(|BST|, |BTS|)

So the inter loss is a real reconstruction error of the graph object — those edges existed and carried weight. But:

  • It is induced entirely by the partition (grouping across layers). A layer-contiguous / order-convex partition creates no 2-cycles and pays zero inter loss. The layer-decay multiplier already pushes toward this.
  • It corresponds to no change in steering behavior (Section 1). It measures how unfaithful the drawing is, not how unfaithful the model summary is.

On "the effects cancel out because they make cycles"

Strictly, there is no cancellation and there are no real cycles — the model is feed-forward; the 2-cycle exists only in the collapsed view. The correct version of your intuition is a clamp-override argument:

  • The intervention is a clamp (set feature = value; see L667, delta = value − current). If you clamp all members of S, then the dropped T→S edges feed into S-members that you have just overridden — so their contribution to those members is moot. In that narrow sense the weaker direction "doesn't matter."
  • This holds for a full clamp of the supernode. It is weaker for additive steering, and it does not apply to the kept S→T direction (T is not clamped, so S→T genuinely propagates).
  • So the dominant-direction heuristic has a partial steering justification only by accident — it drops the direction that points into the clamped set. It is chosen by |weight|, not by clamp structure, so it is not principled, just usually harmless.

Recommendation for the inter term

Keep it, but move it out of "conservation/fidelity" and into structural well-formedness: it is a soft penalty measuring how much 2-cycle mass the partition creates, i.e. how badly the partition fights the topological order. That makes it a partition-quality regularizer (a soft version of F1), reported honestly as drawing-fidelity. Do not claim it predicts intervention error.

The one aggregation effect that is a real misrepresentation of net linear flow is sign cancellation inside a block (opposing-sign edges summing toward zero in BST). That genuinely distorts the linear total effect, and it is already penalized by the coherence / within-block-variance term. Keep it distinct from DAG-forcing.

5. Bonus issue — intra-cluster edge weight as "loss"

You defined Lintra = coherence + (edge weight between nodes inside a cluster). The second part conflicts with the very principle you are borrowing from causal-DAG summarization:

  • In the markdown, intra-cluster edges are reconstructed exactly (the canonical graph keeps original weights inside a cluster) — they contribute zero loss. Strong mutual weights are the restoration benefit that rewards merging.
  • If you instead count intra-cluster weight as lost, you penalize exactly the strong internal connections that justify grouping the nodes — contradicting your own merge criterion.

Recommendation: treat within-supernode structure as intentionally abstracted, not lost. Lintra = coherence only (role disagreement among members). A reader accepts that a supernode hides internal wiring; that is the point of a supernode, not an error.

6. Recommended factoring

L = λcoh·Lcoh (D1, role incoherence within supernodes)
  + λcons·Lprune (D2, 1 − 𝒞: real causal mass lost to pruning)
  + λcplx·Lcplx (D3, K / |V′mid|)
  + μ·Ltopo (structural: 2-cycle mass from cross-layer grouping — soft F1, NOT conservation)
  • Coherence absorbs sign-cancellation and "should these merge" — it is the partition-quality core.
  • Conservation = pruning completeness only. This is the behaviorally-meaningful fidelity term and the one you can cross-check with eval_intervention.
  • DAG-forcing becomes a small structural regularizer μ·Ltopo, reported as drawing-fidelity, separate from the causal story.
  • Keep the optimization objective in ground truth (A) for cheapness; keep steering/intervention as the external (B) validity check. State the split explicitly so reviewers don't read the inter/topo term as a causal claim.
