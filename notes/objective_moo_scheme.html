Summary

You already have the three losses (Lcplx, Latom, Lcausal), an exact MILP solver, and steering + objective baselines. The gap is framing, and the framing has one job: replace "we picked λ and ran a clusterer" with "we characterise the achievable trade-off surface and let an objective-blind causal/interpretability test pick the operating point." That is what turns a chain of heuristics into a method.

Three moves do it:

  1. Demote complexity (D1) to a constraint, not a weight. "Fits in one sitting" is a budget K ≤ Kmax, not a soft cost. This kills one λ and collapses the problem to a 2-objective trade-off (atomicity vs. causal) you can draw as a single Pareto curve — far easier to defend than a point on a 3-simplex.
  2. Optimise by ε-constraint, report the Pareto front. Don't search for "the right λ". Sweep the constraint, trace the front with the exact ILP, and summarise method quality by hypervolume / dominance, not a single L. The ε-constraint scalarisation also (a) reaches concave parts of the front that weighted-sum provably cannot, and (b) is a one-line addition to your current MILP.
  3. Select λ (equivalently, the front point) with the neutral eval. Steering faithfulness and LLM coherence are not in the objective, so using them to pick the operating point is non-circular. Each desideratum gets an external ground-truth metric; the proxy losses are validated against them, then the front point that maximises them is reported as the recommended summary.

One honesty item to put in the paper up front: the MILP optimises a linear surrogate (medoid distance for atomicity, intra-cluster edge mass for causal), while you report the non-linear silhouette and full causal loss. "Exact" is exact for the surrogate. Treat the surrogate↔report agreement as a measured claim, §A.4.

A · Formulating the objective

A.1 Why the weighted sum on the simplex is the part that looks heuristic

Eq. (Lsum) scalarises three losses with λ on the probability simplex. Three structural problems make a single λ hard to defend to a reviewer:

  • Non-interpretable units. λ trades off a silhouette deficit against an edge-mass fraction against a node-count ratio. Equal weights (1/3 each, your default) is a statement about nothing — the losses have different curvatures and dynamic ranges, so 1/3 does not mean "balanced".
  • Convex-hull blindness. Minimising a weighted sum can only ever return points on the convex hull of the Pareto front. If the interesting summary (e.g. the diamond case where one extra supernode buys a lot of causal fidelity) sits in a concave dent, no λ reaches it. This is a theorem, not a tuning problem.
  • Circular tuning. Picking λ to make L look good is choosing the scoreboard to win it. The fix is to never tune λ against L at all (§C).

A.2 The reframe: a constrained 2-objective program

Keep the feasible set exactly as the formulation already pins it down (forced singletons, dominant-direction rule, post-hoc DAG projection). Change only how the three desiderata enter:

DesideratumOld roleNew roleWhy
D1 Complexitysoft loss λcplxLcplxhard constraint K ≤ KmaxInterpretability is a capacity limit ("one sitting"), which is natively a budget. You already have this as constraint (C4) sum_m z[m,m] ≤ max_sn.
D2 Atomicitysoft loss λatomLatomobjective (minimise)Genuine quality axis; no natural absolute threshold.
D3 Causalsoft loss λcausalLcausalobjective or ε-boundGenuine quality axis; also has a natural budget reading ("lose ≤ ε of edge mass").

This leaves a clean bi-objective problem over partitions φ within a complexity budget. The ε-constraint form (the one that reaches the whole front):

minφ Latom(φ) s.t. K(φ)≤Kmax, Lcausal(φ)≤ε

Sweep (Kmax, ε) → trace the Pareto front. Symmetric form (min Lcausal s.t. Latom ≤ δ) traces the same front from the other axis; do both and take the union to be robust to ties.

You can keep Eq. (Lsum) in the paper as the scalar you report for a single chosen summary, but it is no longer how you search. The object of study becomes the front, not L.

A.3 Why ε-constraint is the right scalarisation here (and cheap)

  • Completeness. ε-constraint returns every Pareto-optimal point, including concave-region ones the weighted sum misses. For a discrete partition problem the front is non-convex in general, so this matters.
  • Interpretable knobs. "≤ 10 supernodes, lose ≤ 15% of edge mass, then be as atomic as possible" is a sentence a reviewer accepts. "λ = (0.33, 0.33, 0.34)" is not.
  • One-line ILP change. In cluster_graph_ilp the causal term is already a linear function of the same-cluster indicators y[i,j] (coefficients causal_scale * w). To make it an ε-constraint instead of an objective term, move that row into the constraint matrix: Σ y[i,j]·w[i,j] ≤ ε·W_total. K ≤ Kmax already exists. You then minimise the atomicity term alone.

A.4 honesty The surrogate-vs-report gap, made into a claim

Your MILP does not minimise the reported L. It minimises:

  • Atomicity: mean rectified-cosine distance to the assigned medoid — a one-sided, k-medoids surrogate. The reported compute_L_atom is the silhouette (b−a)/max(a,b), which also rewards separation and is non-linear (min/max over neighbours) — not MILP-able.
  • Causal: intra-cluster edge mass only (mechanism i). The reported compute_L_causal also charges the dominant-direction drop (ii) and sign cancellation (iii), which depend on the post-projection summary adjacency.

Don't hide this — measure it. Across the full sweep, plot surrogate-objective vs. reported-L (and per-term). If they correlate tightly (Spearman ρ near 1), you have earned the sentence "the linear surrogate is a faithful, exactly-solvable stand-in for the reported objective." If they don't, the medoid surrogate is the thing to fix, and that finding is itself a contribution.

Surrogate upgrade worth considering (optional, only if A.4 shows a gap): the medoid distance penalises within-cluster spread but not under-merging. A correlation-clustering objective on the same y[i,j] variables captures both sides like the silhouette claims to:

Σi<j yij · (dij − θ)    with triangle/transitivity constraints   yij + yjk − yik ≤ 1

dij < θ ⇒ merging is rewarded (similar pair); dij > θ ⇒ merging is penalised. θ is a resolution knob analogous to modularity resolution. This shares variables with the causal term and folds atomicity + causal into one clique-partitioning ILP; keep the representative variables z[m,m] only to read off K for the K ≤ Kmax budget. Treat this as a v2 — your current medoid ILP is fine to publish if A.4 holds.

A.5 The "not a chain of heuristics" spine (say this explicitly)

State the pipeline as a sequence of removed heuristics — this is the rhetorical payload:

  1. Desiderata D1–D3 → formal losses in [0,1] (done).
  2. Losses → a constrained program on a feasible set fixed by construction, not by a rule (done; tighten via §A.2).
  3. Solve exactly (ILP with an optimality certificate) instead of greedily — no "merge most-similar pair until k" heuristic in the loop.
  4. Don't hand-pick λ: characterise the whole Pareto front and pick the operating point by an external causal+interpretability test (§C). The only free choice that remains is a stated capacity budget Kmax.

B · Rethinking the baselines

B.1 What baselines are for under this framing

Once the front is the object, a baseline's job is precise: show where it lands relative to the exact front, at matched budget. "Spectral/agglomerative don't optimise this objective" is not a disqualification — it is exactly the point. They become the heuristic frontier that the exact ILP is shown to dominate. So keep them, but change how they're scored: evaluate every method on the same losses, at matched K, and overlay them on the ILP front.

B.2 A four-tier baseline ladder

TierMethodsQuestion it answers
0 · Boundsall-singletons; all-one-cluster; random partition at matched K (seeded)Are the losses even meaningful? Brackets the achievable range; random-at-K is the honest "is structure being used" floor.
1 · Objective-agnostic heuristicsyour cluster_graph_spectral (arith/harm/geo × decay grid), cluster_graph_agglomerative, and the eval_cluster.py trio: baseline-kmeans, baseline-spectral-cosine, baseline-modularityHow far does a clusterer that doesn't know the objective fall short of the front? These are your "chain of heuristics" — now plotted as dominated points.
2 · Same-objective, approximate solvergreedy correlation clustering / Louvain at matched resolution on the linearised objectiveIsolates the value of exactness: same objective, approximate vs. ILP-optimal. Quantifies the optimality gap a heuristic leaves on the table.
3 · Exact (ours)cluster_graph_ilp via ε-constraint sweepThe front itself.
4 · Domain / humanmanual Anthropic-style grouping; LLM auto-grouping (summarization/group_llm.py)External sanity check: does the optimiser agree with how a human/LLM would group, and is it more faithful?

Tier 2 is the most persuasive new baseline and the one currently missing: it separates "right objective" from "exact solve". Without it a reviewer can't tell whether your wins come from the formulation or from the solver.

B.3 Apples-to-apples rules

  • Match the budget. Compare all methods at the same K (or sweep K for each and compare curves). Your eval_cluster.py currently lets each solver pick its own argmin-L K — drop that for the comparison and instead report each method's curve over K, so the comparison is a curve-vs-curve, not point-vs-point.
  • Fix Stage 1. Hold the pruned graph constant (one pruning level, e.g. flow-completeness C ≥ 0.8) so Stage-2 differences aren't confounded by Lprune. Report robustness across 2–3 pruning levels separately.
  • Summarise the front with one number. Hypervolume dominated below a common reference point (worst Latom, worst Lcausal) is the standard MOO scalar: ILP should have the largest hypervolume; heuristics smaller; bounds smallest. Also report the fraction of each heuristic's points that are Pareto-dominated by the ILP front.

C · Evaluation

C.1 The one principle: the referee is not the objective

L is a proxy computed on the graph. The evaluation must be computed on the model and must not be a term you optimised — otherwise selecting λ by it is circular. Steering and LLM scores satisfy this. Structure the eval as a 2×2: each desideratum's proxy loss is validated by an external metric, then the external metrics select the operating point.

C.2 Steering eval as a prediction test (the strong version)

Your eval_steering.py already ablates each supernode and measures ΔP(target), and transplants donor supernodes across BATS analogies. Upgrade it from "supernodes are steerable" to "the summary graph predicts steering outcomes", which is what D3 actually claims:

  • For a perturbation of supernode S, use GSN's edges (the visible WSN) to predict the effect on each downstream supernode / on the logits.
  • Run the real intervention; correlate predicted vs. actual effect. The summary's prediction error is the faithfulness metric.
  • The validating plot: across the Pareto front, prediction error should rise with Lcausal. If it does, Lcausal is a justified proxy and minimising it buys real faithfulness — that single correlation retires the "is the proxy meaningful" question.

Keep both existing modes: ablate (negate members, watch P(target) collapse) tests whether a supernode carries the concept; steer (donor transplant, sweep α, top-1 flip rate) tests whether it is a reusable mechanism. Add the prediction-error metric on top.

C.3 LLM eval, with the bias controls reviewers will demand

  • Per-supernode coherence: give the judge the member features' top activations + Neuronpedia labels; ask for a single-sentence role and a 1–5 coherence/monosemanticity score. Mean over supernodes ↔ atomicity.
  • Graph-level story: give the whole GSN; ask the judge to produce the causal narrative; score whether it is coherent and whether it predicts held-out behaviour ↔ complexity/legibility.
  • Controls: blind to method; randomise supernode/option order; ≥2 judge models (e.g. one Gemini, one GPT — both keys are in config.py); report inter-judge agreement; spot-check against human labels on a small slice. An LLM judge with no agreement number is not evidence.

C.4 Closing the loop: choosing the operating point without tuning λ

  1. Trace the front (ε-constraint sweep, exact ILP), at fixed Stage-1 pruning.
  2. For each front point compute the external composite (steering faithfulness + LLM coherence). These were never optimised, so this is non-circular.
  3. Report the front point that maximises the external composite as the recommended summary, and back out the implied λ (or the (Kmax, ε) it corresponds to). Show this choice is stable across prompts/datasets/2 models — stability is the claim that it's a method, not a fit.
  4. λ-free fallback when no eval is available: the front's knee (max-curvature / closest-to-ideal point) is a principled default. Report knee vs. eval-selected; if they nearly coincide, even better.
