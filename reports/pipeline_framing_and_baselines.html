Three-stage summarization: optimization framing & baseline design

Methodology review of Chapters 3 & 5 — reconciling the "single optimization problem" story with the staged solver, and a baseline suite for a novel task. 2026-06-08.

Summary

  • The framing tension is real but cosmetic, not structural. You don't have "three stages each optimizing its own objective." You have two optimizers (prune, cluster) over nested decision variables V′ ⊃ f, plus one deterministic constructor (interpret) that enforces the DAG constraint. Stage 3 optimizes nothing — saying so removes most of the awkwardness.
  • Fix the story, keep the method. State one joint objective over (V′, f), declare the solver greedy / lexicographic (justified by intractability of the joint problem), and own the greedy gap as a one-paragraph limitation. Don't rebuild the pipeline as a single solver.
  • One genuine inconsistency to flag: C1 (acyclicity) is a constraint in §3.4 but is enforced after clustering. So the ILP minimizes the clustering loss over a cyclic-allowed relaxation, and stage-3 back-edge deletion discards between-supernode mass that no loss accounts for. This is a hidden fourth source of infidelity — name it.
  • Baselines: the task is new, so there is no rival system. The correct design is therefore floor (random), oracle (human supernodes), and per-stage swaps at matched budget — not a competing end-to-end method. This is standard for a new task and is a strength of the framing.
  • Biggest single upgrade to Chapter 5: replace the single-K table with a faithfulness-vs-complexity Pareto curve (you already have pareto_curve.py). It dissolves both biases you already flagged — "K-means hides its weakest edges" and "K-means is graded on its own objective."

Part 1 — The optimization framing (your Q2)

Diagnosis: a formulation/solver mismatch

§3.4 reads as a single constrained problem — "the pipeline searches for a summary graph that minimizes the desiderata losses subject to C1–C2" — but the method solves it in three sequential passes. Four specific gaps make the single-problem reading feel false:

  1. There is no scalar linking Lprune to the clustering loss. You give λ (atom vs causal) but nothing weighing pruning against clustering. So a single summed objective Ltotal doesn't actually exist in the text. What you really do is lexicographic: fix V′ by minimizing Lprune, then minimize Latom + λLcausal given V′. That is a legitimate framing — but it's an order, not a sum.
  2. Stage 3 is not an optimizer. Given f, the block-sum → antiparallel-collapse → back-edge-removal map is deterministic. WH = Interpret(f). Calling it a third "stage that optimizes its own objective" is what creates the "three competing objectives" discomfort. It's a constraint-enforcing construction, full stop.
  3. C1 is declared a constraint but enforced post-hoc. §3.4 says admissible H must be acyclic; §3.5 then says "the acyclicity constraint is deferred to the interpreting stage." So the ILP's feasible set is not the admissible set of §3.4 — it minimizes over partitions whose block-sum adjacency may be cyclic, and stage 3 repairs them by deleting edges.
  4. Back-edge deletion drops mass that no loss tracks. Lcausal counts only within-supernode absorbed weight (f(u)=f(v)). Back-edge removal deletes between-supernode edges. These are disjoint, so Lcausal stays consistent — but the deleted cross-cluster edges are real causal structure thrown away for acyclicity, and they appear in no desideratum. That's a genuine, unmodelled infidelity.

Recommended resolution — stage-specific proxies for one true objective

Supersedes the joint-objective patch below. This is the preferred framing: more honest, and it makes Chapter 5's logic airtight.

Stop presenting the stages as three separately-optimal subproblems jointly judged by one metric. Instead: the true objective — a summary that is faithful and interpretable — cannot be optimized directly (it requires interventions and an LLM at optimization time). So each stage minimizes a cheap, computable proxy for it, and Chapter 5 measures the true objective directly to check the proxies worked. This is the standard surrogate-loss story.

(a) Name the true objective, two-dimensional. Faithfulness (causal) and interpretability (readability/relevance). The four desiderata are proxies or constraints for these — and each has a matching direct measurement, which is what earns the word "proxy":

(b) Split constraints from proxies. DAG-ness and K ≤ Kmax are constraints on what counts as a summary at all — Interpret enforces them, it does not minimize a loss. This keeps the interpret stage honest (it is a constructor, not an optimizer) and removes the "three competing objectives" problem at the root.

(c) Keep the optimization rigor. You recast the status of the stage losses (proxies for a downstream-validated goal), not their content. The ILP is still the exact minimizer of a well-defined proxy. So you get honesty and rigor: "each stage exactly or approximately optimizes a well-defined proxy for the faithfulness and interpretability we then measure directly."

(d) The K-means result becomes the finding, not the confound. Under this lens, K-means beating the ILP at λ=1 means its Euclidean role-cohesion proxy tracks the steering metric better than the correlation-clustering proxy. "Graded on its own objective" stops being an apology and becomes the measured point — which proxy best serves the true objective.

Two caveats to keep honest

  • Interpret is not only readability. The edge construction WH (block-sum + antiparallel collapse) is a deterministic, faithfulness-relevant map — it is exactly what the intervention test grades. Describe Interpret as "a deterministic faithfulness-relevant construction of WH, plus enforcement of the readability constraints," not readability alone.
  • The DAG constraint can cost faithfulness. Back-edge deletion drops cross-supernode mass tracked by no loss. Cleanly framed now: a place where the interpretability constraint trades against the faithfulness objective — surface it, don't hide it.

The cost, and the fallback

You give up the "single grand optimization problem" aesthetic — but §3.4 never delivered it (no scalar μ links Lprune to the clustering loss). A research-track committee rewards honest structure + clean eval logic over a leaky unification. Keep the joint-objective version below only as a one-line "alternative" if a reviewer insists on a single objective.

Alternative (superseded): one joint objective + greedy solver
minimize  Lprune(V′)  +  μ · [ Latom(f) + λ · Lcausal(f) ]   over (V′, f),   s.t.  |VH| ≤ Kmax (C2),  Interpret(f) acyclic (C1).

State the joint problem as the in-principle target, declare the solver greedy/lexicographic (V* = argmin Lprune; then f* = argmin Latom+λLcausal; H = Interpret(f*) deterministic), justify by intractability, and own the greedy gap. Coherent, but less honest than the proxy framing because it implies a unification the method does not perform.

Part 2 — Baselines for a 3-stage pipeline (your Q1)

Principle 1 — A new task has no rival system

There is no competing automated attribution-graph summarizer to compare against; that's the contribution. So the baseline design is necessarily ablation + oracle + floor, which is exactly right for a new task. Three roles:

  • floor — random / trivial: shows the method beats chance.
  • oracle — human-labelled supernodes (Ameisen et al. multi-hop): the ceiling and the human anchor for your LLM-score validation.
  • swap — replace one stage, hold the other two at your setting: isolates each design choice.

Principle 2 — Match the budget the metric is sensitive to

You already do this for clustering (match K). Generalize it: every baseline is matched to your method on the resource its metric reacts to — K for clustering, |V′| (or retained mass) for pruning — so the comparison isolates the algorithm, not the granularity.

Principle 3 — Report a curve, not a point

Your Chapter 5 already concedes the single-K table is biased two ways: K-means "hides its weakest, most ambiguous edges inside clusters" and is "partly graded on its own objective." Both vanish if you sweep complexity and plot faithfulness (SC, ρ, |Δp|) vs K and vs #edges. A method that exposes fewer edges simply sits at a different point on the same curve — no longer an unfair advantage. You have the harness (pareto_curve.py already sweeps λ); extend it to sweep K for every clusterer.

Mapping to your two metrics

Nodes → LLM relevance score. Node-level baselines (prune swaps, random vs human supernodes) are graded by how relevant the LLM judges each supernode. The human supernodes are the key anchor here: your Chapter-5 draft already wants LLM-vs-human agreement, so the oracle does double duty — it's both a clustering baseline and the validation target for the LLM score itself.

Edges → intervention. Cluster and interpret swaps are graded by SC / ρ / |Δp| (your eval_intervention.py). The singletons ceiling is the most informative addition: it is maximally edge-faithful (nothing absorbed), so the gap from singletons to your method is the faithfulness cost of abstraction — currently invisible in the ILP-vs-K-means table.
