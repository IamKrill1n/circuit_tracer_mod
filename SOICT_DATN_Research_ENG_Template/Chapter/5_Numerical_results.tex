\section{Overview}
\section{Dataset}
\subsection{Synthetic Analogies from BATS}
The Bigger Analogy Test Set (BATS) is a widely used benchmark for word embeddings and language models to capture relational and semantic knowledge.
We extract the entities in BATS lexicography category to create a synthetic dataset consist of 10 prompts per subcategory. The prompts in the dataset are of the format:
The saying goes: A is to B as C is to D 
Where A, B, C, D are entities in the BATS lexicography subcategory. The task is to predict D given A, B, and C.
\subsection{Multihop reasoning dataset}
We follow the multihop queries in \cite{circuit_tracer}. The dataset contains multiple queries that require reasoning across multiple steps to arrive at the correct answer. Each query is designed to test the model's ability to perform basic reasoning and inference. 

All the prompts in both datasets are chosen such that the model correctly output the answer with confidence > 20\%.
\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Environment Setup}
Experiments are conducted on a single PC with NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU, 24GB VRAM, and 64GB RAM. The operating system is Ubuntu 22.04 LTS. We use Python 3.10 for all implementations and experiments.
\subsection{Tools and Libraries}
We use the circuit tracer library to generate the attribution graphs for our prompts. We also utilize features' metadata provided by the library and adapt the UI to visualize the attribution graphs. 
\section{Evaluation Methodology}
\subsection{Baseline Methods}
\subsection{LLM score}
We utilize an LLM to both label and score the supernodes in a summary graph.
The scoring process is the opposite of the labeling process. We first prompt the LLM to label the supernodes in the summary graph, then we prompt the LLM to score each supernode based on its relevance to the final answer. The scoring is done on a scale of 0 to 1, where 0 indicates that the supernode is not relevant at all, and 1 indicates that the supernode is highly relevant to the final answer. The LLM's score for each supernode is then used as a measure of its importance in the reasoning process.
\subsection{Steering evaluation}

Dữ liệu xử lý thế nào? … Có thể chia  chương này thành các chương nhỏ hơn để tiện trình bày.

A summary graph is faithful only if its supernode edges reflect genuine causal
dependencies in the replacement model. We test this directly with a steering
intervention: we perturb one supernode at its source and check whether every other
supernode moves in the direction its incoming edge predicts. The experiment grades
the Stage~2 partition together with the Stage~3 edges $W_H$ at once, so it scores
the summary graph as a whole rather than any single stage.

\paragraph{Intervention protocol.}
For each non-fixed supernode $C$ of a summary graph we negate every member feature,
setting its activation to $M \cdot a$ with the steer factor $M = -1$ and $a$ the
original activation, then run one forward pass with all members of $C$ steered
together. The perturbation is applied as \emph{constrained} (direct-effect)
patching, following the methodology of~\cite{ameisen2025circuit}: each steered
feature writes its decoder vector only over the layer window $[l, l+1]$ around its
own layer $l$ --- its own-layer reconstruction plus the first cross-layer write ---
while the attention patterns and LayerNorm scales are frozen at their clean values.
Freezing attention and normalization isolates the \emph{direct} effect that the
summary edges model and suppresses the indirect paths the attribution graph does
not represent.

\paragraph{Measured response.}
After the intervention we read the activation change $\Delta a_{C'}$ of every other
feature supernode $C'$ and the probability change $\Delta p_{C'}$ of every logit
supernode and of the target token. For a steered source $C$ and a downstream
supernode $C'$ joined by the summary edge $W_{H,\,C \to C'}$, the edge
\emph{predicts} that the downstream response carries the sign
$\operatorname{sgn}(W_{H,\,C \to C'}) \cdot \operatorname{sgn}(\Delta a_C)$, where
$\Delta a_C$ is the activation change of the steered supernode itself (negative
under negation).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
We summarize each graph with three quantities, all oriented so that higher is
better. \emph{Edge sign-consistency} is the fraction of summary edges whose measured
downstream sign matches the predicted one, weighted by edge magnitude:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SC} \;=\;
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{(C, C') \in E_H} |W_{H,\,C \to C'}| \cdot
      \mathbf{1}\!\bigl[\operatorname{sgn}(\Delta m_{C'}) =
      \operatorname{sgn}(W_{H,\,C \to C'})\,\operatorname{sgn}(\Delta a_C)\bigr]}
     {\displaystyle\sum_{(C, C') \in E_H} |W_{H,\,C \to C'}|}
\;\in\; [0, 1],
\label{eq:sign-consistency}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta m_{C'}$ is $\Delta a_{C'}$ for a feature target and $\Delta p_{C'}$ for
a logit target; $\mathrm{SC} = 1$ means every edge fires in the predicted direction.
\emph{Monotonicity} is the Spearman correlation $\rho$ between the edge magnitude
$|W_{H,\,C \to C'}|$ and the measured effect magnitude over the feature-to-feature
edges, testing whether heavier edges produce larger downstream changes.
\emph{Output effect} is the mean absolute target-token probability shift
$|\Delta p|$ across all single-supernode interventions, measuring how much causal
weight a supernode carries toward the model's answer.

\paragraph{Baseline and parameters.}
We compare the exact ILP clusterer (Section~\ref{sec:objective}) against a
$K$-means baseline that partitions the same role vectors $r(u)$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:role} with Euclidean $K$-means. The ILP chooses its supernode count
$K$ endogenously, whereas $K$-means takes $K$ as input; we therefore set the
baseline's $K$ equal to the ILP's $K$ on each graph, so the two methods differ only
in how they assign features and not in granularity, making the comparison fair at a
fixed budget. The experiment runs on the $100$ pruned attribution graphs from the
analogies dataset (softmax token weighting, pruning $\alpha = 0.5$, node threshold
$\eta_V = 0.02$). Each graph is processed once: the intervention is deterministic
given a partition, the ILP is solved to optimality ($\theta = 0$, $\lambda = 1$,
layer span $\le 4$, $30$\,s time limit), and $K$-means uses $20$ restarts under a
fixed seed.

\section{Validate summary graph with interventions}

Both clusterers produce causally faithful summary graphs: negating a supernode moves
its downstream neighbours in the predicted direction for the large majority of edges,
and the effect grows with edge weight. Table~\ref{tab:intervention} reports the two
methods at equal granularity.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\caption{Steering-intervention faithfulness on the $100$ analogy graphs, ILP versus a
$K$-matched $K$-means baseline. Values are means over the $100$ graphs; arrows mark
the direction of improvement and bold the better method per column. The three causal
columns ---sign-consistency $\mathrm{SC}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:sign-consistency}),
monotonicity $\rho$, and output effect $|\Delta p|$--- all favour $K$-means, and every
per-graph difference is significant (paired Wilcoxon $p < 0.01$). The right-most column
is diagnostic: the mean number of supernode edges left visible in $H$, a measure of
exposed structure rather than of quality. $K$ is identical for the two methods by
construction.}
\label{tab:intervention}
\begin{tabular}{l c c c c c}
\toprule
Clusterer & $K$ & $\mathrm{SC}\uparrow$ & $\rho\uparrow$ & $|\Delta p|\uparrow$ & edges/graph \\
\midrule
ILP ($\lambda = 1$)            & 15.3 & 0.878 & 0.442 & 0.175 & 14.6 \\
$K$-means (role vectors)       & 15.3 & \textbf{0.941} & \textbf{0.539} & \textbf{0.190} & 13.0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

At equal $K$ the $K$-means baseline is the more faithful of the two on every causal
metric. Its edges are more sign-consistent ($0.941$ vs.\ $0.878$), more monotone
($\rho = 0.539$ vs.\ $0.442$), and its supernodes shift the target probability more
($0.190$ vs.\ $0.175$); all three gaps hold across the $100$ graphs at
$p < 0.01$. The pooled, edge-weighted sign-consistency over all $4{,}799$ tested
supernode pairs tells the same story ($0.927$ vs.\ $0.861$).

The one axis on which the ILP leads is the amount of causal structure it exposes: it
leaves $14.6$ supernode edges visible per graph against the baseline's $13.0$. This is
the intended effect of the causal-preservation loss $L_{\text{causal}}$ (D3), which
penalizes merging directly connected features and so keeps their edge in $H$ instead of
absorbing it into a supernode. The same mechanism explains the sign-consistency gap:
the ILP surfaces more --- and individually weaker --- edges and is then graded on all of
them, whereas $K$-means hides its weakest, most ambiguous edges inside clusters and is
graded only on the strong ones that remain. The comparison therefore measures per-edge
faithfulness at fixed $K$, not the overall quality of the abstraction, and it is partly
biased toward the method that exposes less structure.

Two caveats further temper the result. First, $K$-means optimizes role-vector cohesion
directly, which is close to what the steering metric rewards, so it is partly graded on
its own objective. Second, the comparison fixes the trade-off weight at $\lambda = 1$,
and that weight is precisely the knob moving the ILP along the structure-versus-cohesion
axis; a sweep over $\lambda$ is needed to locate the operating point at which the two
effects balance. We conclude that both abstractions are faithful and that, at this
single operating point, the simple baseline is at least as faithful per edge while the
ILP retains more of the circuit's causal wiring.
