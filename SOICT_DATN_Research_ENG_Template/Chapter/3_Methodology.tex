\section{Overview}

The previous chapter established that an attribution graph faithfully records how a
language model computes a single prediction, but that reading such a graph by hand does
not scale. This chapter presents the method that removes that bottleneck: an automated
pipeline that turns an attribution graph --- the circuit a replacement model induces for
one prompt --- into a small, human-readable \emph{summary graph} of supernodes. The
pipeline has three stages. \textbf{Pruning} discards the nodes and edges that do not
carry the model's behaviour, including every reconstruction-error node, leaving a compact
subgraph. \textbf{Clustering} groups the surviving feature nodes into supernodes that each
read as a single mechanism. \textbf{Interpreting} forces the supernode graph to be a
directed acyclic graph and labels each supernode, so the reader can follow the causal
story at a glance. Section~\ref{sec:problem} first formalizes the task as finding a summary
graph of an attribution graph and states the desiderata, objectives, and constraints each
stage must meet; Section~\ref{sec:method} then presents the three stages in turn.
Figure~\ref{fig:methodology-pipeline} previews the whole pipeline on the running
``Dallas $\to$ Austin'' example.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Input}
An attribution graph is a directed acyclic graph $G = (V, E, W)$ whose vertex
set is partitioned into
$V = V_{\text{emb}} \cup V_{\text{inter}} \cup V_{\text{error}} \cup V_{\text{logit}}$
(embedding, intermediate, error, and logit nodes), with signed weights
$W \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ on $N := |V|$ nodes, where $W_{ij}$ is the linear
influence of source node $j$ on target node $i$. Embedding and error nodes are
sources (no incoming edges), logit nodes are sinks (no outgoing edges), and each
intermediate node carries signal between them, indexed by a transformer
(layer, position) pair; a node may only have edges to nodes of a higher layer.
A token weight $\tau \in \mathbb{R}^N$ with $\sum_e \tau_e = 1$ marks the input
tokens of interest and a logit weight $\ell \in \mathbb{R}^N$ marks the output
positions of interest; both enter the loss terms below.

\subsection{Output}
A \emph{summary graph} is a directed acyclic graph $H = (V_H,\, E_H,\, W_H,\, f)$,
where $V_H$ is the set of supernodes, $(E_H,\, W_H)$ are the edges between them
with signed weights, and $f$ is the partition map from the pruned node set $V'$ to
$V_H$. The three stages produce its parts in turn.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Pruning} produces the pruned subgraph $V' \subseteq V$,
$E' \subseteq E \cap (V' \times V')$. Pruning removes every error node, so $V'$
contains only embedding, feature, and logit nodes.
\item \textbf{Clustering} produces the partition map $f : V' \to V_H$
assigning each surviving node to a supernode. Its image
$V_H = \{C_1, C_2, \dots, C_K\}$ is the node set of $H$ --- a partition of $V'$
into $K := |V_H|$ supernodes. Every embedding and logit node is its own
singleton supernode; only the feature nodes
$V'_{\text{mid}} := V' \cap V_{\text{inter}}$ are free to be grouped.
\item \textbf{Interpreting} produces the edges $E_H$ and their signed
adjacency $W_H$, forces $H$ to be a DAG, and labels each supernode for display.
\end{itemize}
The construction of $W_H$ from the pruned edges is given in
Section~\ref{sec:stage3}.

\subsection{Desiderata of the summary graph}
A reader inspects $H$ to recover the model's causal story, so the summary should
be \emph{interpretable} (the flow of information can be traced at a glance) and
\emph{faithful} ($H$ accurately represents the causal structure of the attribution
graph). Four criteria translate these requirements into measurable conditions.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.0em,style=nextline,topsep=2pt,itemsep=3pt]
\item[(D1) Retain important nodes and edges.] The pruned subgraph $(V', E')$
should keep the nodes and edges that carry the causal flow from $V_{\text{emb}}$
to $V_{\text{logit}}$ in $G$, and discard the rest. A node or edge is important
when it lies on a high influence--relevance pathway
(Eqs.~\eqref{eq:inf}--\eqref{eq:rel}); pruning away unimportant structure makes
the summary tractable without changing what the model does.
\item[(D2) Atomicity.] Members of a supernode share a single causal role ---
similar influential downstream targets and similar relevant upstream sources ---
so the supernode reads as one mechanism, and the partition cannot be improved by
further splitting or merging.
\item[(D3) Causal-information preservation.] The summary should keep the causal
relationships between pruned nodes. An edge between two members of the same
supernode is collapsed into the node and so disappears from $H$; in general,
nodes joined by strong paths should therefore not be merged, as merging them makes
the summary less faithful.
\item[(D4) Interpretability.] $H$ must be a DAG with few enough supernodes to
inspect in one sitting. The DAG structure is a hard constraint, since causal
relationships cannot be read off a graph that contains cycles.
\end{description}

\subsection{Objectives and constraints}\label{sec:objective}
The pipeline searches for a summary graph that minimizes the desiderata losses subject
to two hard constraints that make $H$ interpretable (D4); a candidate $H$ is admissible
only if it satisfies both.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.0em,style=nextline,topsep=2pt,itemsep=3pt]
\item[(C1) Acyclicity.] $H$ must be a directed acyclic graph: its edge set $E_H$
induces no directed cycle. Causal relationships cannot be read off a graph that
contains cycles; this constraint is enforced in the interpreting stage.
\item[(C2) Complexity budget.] The number of supernodes is capped at a budget
$K_{\max}$: $K = |V_H| \le K_{\max}$, so the reader can inspect $H$ in one sitting.
\end{description}

Within the admissible set, the pipeline minimizes three losses, one per
desideratum. Pruning minimizes the pruning loss $L_{\text{prune}}$ (D1), which
depends only on the pruned subgraph $(V', E')$ and is therefore scored separately
(Section~\ref{sec:stage1}). Clustering minimizes the two partition-dependent losses
below: the \emph{atomicity loss} $L_{\text{atom}}$ (D2) and the
\emph{causal-preservation loss} $L_{\text{causal}}$ (D3).

The atomicity loss is the signed-cosine \emph{correlation-clustering} loss over
the free feature nodes. With the same-cluster indicator
$x_{ij} = \mathbf{1}[f(i) = f(j)]$, a resolution threshold $\theta \in [-1, 1]$,
and the role-similarity entries $s_{ij}$ of the matrix $S$ defined in the clustering
stage (Eq.~\eqref{eq:sim}),
\begin{equation}
L_{\text{atom}}(f)
\;=\;
\sum_{\{i, j\} \subseteq V'_{\text{mid}}} x_{ij}\,(\theta - s_{ij}).
\label{eq:Latom}
\end{equation}
A similar pair ($s_{ij} > \theta$) contributes a negative term, so merging it is
rewarded; a dissimilar pair ($s_{ij} < \theta$) contributes a positive term, so
merging it is penalized. Minimizing $L_{\text{atom}}$ therefore penalizes
\emph{over-merging} (dissimilar features placed together) and \emph{under-merging}
(similar features kept apart) at once.

The causal-preservation loss is the fraction of pruned edge weight absorbed
\emph{inside} a supernode --- weight between two members of the same supernode,
which the interpreting stage collapses into the node and which therefore never
appears in $H$:
\begin{equation}
L_{\text{causal}}(f)
\;=\;
\frac{\sum_{(u, v) \in E':\, f(u) = f(v)} |W_{vu}|}
     {\sum_{(u, v) \in E'} |W_{vu}|}
\;\in\; [0, 1].
\label{eq:Lcausal}
\end{equation}
Clustering minimizes the two as a single weighted objective with one trade-off weight
$\lambda \ge 0$ on the causal term,
\begin{equation}
L(f) \;=\; L_{\text{atom}}(f) \;+\; \lambda\, L_{\text{causal}}(f),
\label{eq:Lstage2}
\end{equation}
subject to the constraints (C1)--(C2). The weight $\lambda$ governs a genuine
trade-off: merging role-similar features that are also directly connected improves
atomicity (D2) but hides their edge (D3).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Prune}\label{sec:stage1}
Pruning keeps the nodes and edges that carry the main computation and discards
the rest (D1), leaving a subgraph small enough for the later stages. It scores every
node and edge by importance, keeps the most important ones up to a retained-mass
budget, then removes any node left dangling. We first define the two quantities the
importance score combines.

\paragraph{Influence and relevance.}
With the column-normalized matrix
$W^{\text{in}}_{ij} = |W_{ij}|/\sum_k |W_{kj}|$ and the row-normalized
$W^{\text{out}}_{ij} = |W_{ij}|/\sum_k |W_{ik}|$, the per-node \emph{influence}
(downstream reach to logits) and \emph{relevance} (upstream reach to input
tokens) are
\begin{align}
\mathrm{Inf} &= \ell^\top \bigl(I - (W^{\text{in}})^\top\bigr)^{-1}
= \ell^\top \sum_{k \ge 0} \bigl((W^{\text{in}})^\top\bigr)^k \nonumber\\
&= \ell^\top \Bigl(I + (W^{\text{in}})^\top + \bigl((W^{\text{in}})^\top\bigr)^2 + \cdots\Bigr)
\;\in\; \mathbb{R}^N, \label{eq:inf}\\
\mathrm{Rel} &= \tau^\top \bigl(I - W^{\text{out}}\bigr)^{-1}
= \tau^\top \sum_{k \ge 0} (W^{\text{out}})^k \nonumber\\
&= \tau^\top \bigl(I + W^{\text{out}} + (W^{\text{out}})^2 + \cdots\bigr)
\;\in\; \mathbb{R}^N. \label{eq:rel}
\end{align}
Because $G$ is a DAG, $W^{\text{in}}$ and $W^{\text{out}}$ are nilpotent, so each
Neumann series terminates after finitely many terms and the inverse is
well-defined.

\paragraph{Importance score.}
A node matters to the prompt's computation only if it both reaches the logits and
is reached from the input tokens, so its \emph{importance} combines the two scores.
After normalizing each score to $[0, 1]$ across nodes (by rank or min--max), the
combined importance is the weighted geometric mean
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Imp}_v \;=\;
\widehat{\mathrm{Inf}}_v^{\,\alpha}\,\widehat{\mathrm{Rel}}_v^{\,1 - \alpha},
\qquad \alpha \in [0, 1],
\label{eq:importance}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{\,\cdot\,}$ denotes the normalized score; the arithmetic and
harmonic means are interchangeable alternatives. The geometric mean is the natural
default because it is zero whenever \emph{either} factor is zero: a node that does
not reach the logits, or one that is not reached from the input tokens, scores zero
and is pruned. This is precisely why pruning removes every error node --- an error
node is a source with no incoming edges and no path from the input tokens, so its
relevance is zero, hence its combined importance is zero. The same combiner scores
edges, using the edge-level influence and relevance of their endpoints.

\paragraph{Selection and the pruning loss.}
Pruning keeps the highest-importance nodes whose cumulative importance reaches a
retained-mass fraction $\eta_V \in [0, 1]$, and likewise keeps edges up to a
fraction $\eta_E$; embedding and logit nodes are kept unconditionally. It then
removes \emph{dangling} nodes in a fixed-point sweep --- a source that has lost all
outgoing edges, or a feature or logit that has lost all incoming edges --- until the
subgraph is stable. The thresholds set the pruning loss directly: writing the kept
importance mass as a fraction of the total, the pruning loss (D1) is the discarded
fraction,
\begin{equation}
L_{\text{prune}} \;=\; 1 - \frac{\sum_{v \in V'} \mathrm{Imp}_v}{\sum_{v \in V} \mathrm{Imp}_v}
\;\in\; [0, 1],
\label{eq:Lprune}
\end{equation}
so a higher $\eta_V$ retains more causal mass at the cost of a larger subgraph.
The thresholds are chosen to keep $L_{\text{prune}}$ small while shrinking $|V'|$
enough for the later stages to be tractable.

\subsection{Cluster}\label{sec:stage2}
Clustering groups the surviving feature nodes $V'_{\text{mid}}$ into supernodes that
each read as one mechanism (D2), while keeping enough edge weight visible across
supernodes (D3) and the supernode count small (D4). It compares features by a
\emph{role vector} built from their weighted edge profiles, scores pairs of features
with a role similarity, and partitions them by minimizing the objective of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Lstage2}.

\paragraph{Role vectors.}
The role vector of an intermediate feature stacks its weighted outgoing and
incoming edge profiles, each coordinate scaled by the importance of the
corresponding endpoint:
\begin{align}
(v_u^{\text{out}})_k &= W_{ku}\,\sqrt{\mathrm{Inf}_k}, \quad k \in V', \nonumber\\
(v_u^{\text{in}})_k  &= W_{uk}\,\sqrt{\mathrm{Rel}_k}, \quad k \in V', \nonumber\\
r(u) &= \bigl[\,v_u^{\text{out}}\,;\, v_u^{\text{in}}\,\bigr]
\;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{2|V'|}. \label{eq:role}
\end{align}
The coordinates are \emph{signed} (they inherit the sign of $W$), so two features
that drive the same targets with opposite sign --- an excitatory and an
inhibitory feature --- have opposing role vectors. Outgoing coordinates are
weighted by the downstream importance of the target ($\sqrt{\mathrm{Inf}_k}$),
incoming coordinates by the upstream importance of the source
($\sqrt{\mathrm{Rel}_k}$), so $r(u)$ concentrates on the edge dimensions that
actually carry causal mass.

\paragraph{Role similarity: four variants.}
Atomicity asks that features sharing a supernode have similar role vectors and
features in different supernodes do not. The role-similarity matrix $S$ is the only
place a comparison between features enters the pipeline, so it can be defined in
several ways; we use four. They split into a single signed kernel over the whole role
vector and three nonnegative combinations of two directional kernels.

The first variant is the \emph{signed cosine} of the full role vector, collected into
the symmetric matrix
$S \in \mathbb{R}^{|V'_{\text{mid}}| \times |V'_{\text{mid}}|}$,
\begin{equation}
S^{\cos}_{ij} \;=\; \cos\bigl(r(i), r(j)\bigr) \;\in\; [-1, 1],
\qquad i, j \in V'_{\text{mid}}.
\label{eq:sim}
\end{equation}
It is signed: antagonistic features --- same targets, opposite sign --- have
$S^{\cos}_{ij} < 0$ and are treated as maximally dissimilar, so they repel. This is
also what D3 wants, since merging opposing-sign features cancels mass when their
edges are aggregated into one supernode edge (Eq.~\eqref{eq:block-sum}). The signed
cosine is the similarity that the correlation-clustering loss $L_{\text{atom}}$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Latom} reads, with $s_{ij} := S^{\cos}_{ij}$.

The other three variants take a \emph{multi-kernel} view. They split the role vector
into its outgoing and incoming halves and form two base kernels --- a rectified
cosine of the outgoing profiles, measuring shared downstream targets, and a rectified
cosine of the incoming profiles, measuring shared upstream sources,
\begin{align}
s^{\text{out}}_{ij} &= \max\!\bigl(0,\, \cos(v_i^{\text{out}}, v_j^{\text{out}})\bigr) \in [0, 1], \label{eq:sout}\\
s^{\text{in}}_{ij}  &= \max\!\bigl(0,\, \cos(v_i^{\text{in}},  v_j^{\text{in}})\bigr)  \in [0, 1]. \label{eq:sin}
\end{align}
Each is a positive-semidefinite kernel (the cosine-normalized Gram matrix of the
weighted edge profiles), and the three variants combine them with a different mean,
\begin{equation}
S^{\text{arith}}_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(s^{\text{out}}_{ij} + s^{\text{in}}_{ij}\bigr),
\quad
S^{\text{geo}}_{ij} = \sqrt{s^{\text{out}}_{ij}\, s^{\text{in}}_{ij}},
\quad
S^{\text{harm}}_{ij} = \frac{2\, s^{\text{out}}_{ij}\, s^{\text{in}}_{ij}}{s^{\text{out}}_{ij} + s^{\text{in}}_{ij}},
\label{eq:sim-mk}
\end{equation}
all in $[0, 1]$. The mean sets how strictly a pair must agree on \emph{both}
directions: the harmonic mean is near zero unless both kernels are high (a strict
``and''), the arithmetic mean averages them so strong agreement on one direction can
compensate the other, and the geometric mean lies between. An optional
layer-distance decay $\exp(-\gamma\,|\Delta\text{layer}|)$ and a hard layer-span mask
multiply the combined similarity, discouraging or forbidding merges of features many
layers apart.

The two families differ in sign and in scope. The signed cosine
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:sim}) is one kernel over the concatenated role vector and keeps the
sign, so antagonistic pairs become negative and repel; the multi-kernel scores
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:sim-mk}) are nonnegative and combine the two directions separately.
The choice is dictated by the solver below: the signed cosine feeds the
correlation-clustering objective, which needs a signed similarity to compare against
the threshold $\theta$, whereas the nonnegative multi-kernel scores feed the spectral
and agglomerative clusterers, which require a nonnegative affinity. The resolution
threshold $\theta$ is the only granularity knob for the signed variant, and
$\theta = 0$ is the natural default, since the sign of the cosine then decides whether
a pair should merge.

\paragraph{Exact ILP solver.}
The exact solver chooses the partition $f$ by minimizing the weighted objective
$L(f) = L_{\text{atom}}(f) + \lambda\, L_{\text{causal}}(f)$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Lstage2} on the signed cosine, subject to the complexity budget (C2);
the acyclicity constraint (C1) is deferred to the interpreting stage. Both losses are
linear in the same-cluster indicators $x_{ij}$: $L_{\text{atom}}$ is linear by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Latom}, and the absorbed-mass numerator of $L_{\text{causal}}$ is
$\sum_{(u, v) \in E'} |W_{vu}|\, x_{uv}$, since a feature edge is internal to a
supernode exactly when its endpoints share one. The objective is therefore linear,
and clustering reduces to a binary integer linear program with the following
variables and constraints.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Variables.} A binary $x_{ij} \in \{0, 1\}$ for each feature pair
($x_{ij} = 1$ iff $i, j$ share a supernode), plus binary representative variables
$r_i \in \{0, 1\}$ ($r_i = 1$ iff $i$ is the lowest-indexed member of its
supernode) used only to count $K = \sum_i r_i$.
\item \textbf{Valid partition.} Transitivity constraints
$x_{ij} + x_{jk} - x_{ik} \le 1$ force the indicators to define an equivalence
relation, so the $x_{ij}$ realize an actual partition.
\item \textbf{Complexity budget (C2).} $\sum_i r_i \le K_{\max}$, with the
defining inequalities tying each $r_i$ to its pair variables.
\item \textbf{Layer span.} Feature pairs more than a fixed number of layers apart
are forced to $x_{ij} = 0$; with transitivity this bounds every supernode's layer
span.
\end{itemize}
For the small pruned graphs considered here the program is solved to optimality
with a mixed-integer solver, so the reported summary is the exact minimizer, not
the output of a heuristic.

\paragraph{Scalable clusterers.}
The integer program grows with the number of feature pairs and is tractable only on
small graphs, so two approximate clusterers handle larger ones using the nonnegative
multi-kernel affinity of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sim-mk}. \emph{Spectral} clustering treats the
affinity as a precomputed graph and cuts it into $K$ groups; \emph{agglomerative}
clustering merges the most similar pair of clusters under average linkage while
rejecting any merge that would exceed the layer span or create a cycle in the
supernode graph, so it satisfies the DAG constraint natively. Both select their
cluster count by minimizing the same closed-form objective $L$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Lstage2} over a candidate range, so the three solvers optimize one
criterion and differ only in how they search.

\subsection{Interpret}\label{sec:stage3}
Interpreting turns the partitioned pruned graph into the acyclic summary graph $H$ and
labels its supernodes. It aggregates the pruned edges into a supernode adjacency,
then removes the cycles that aggregation can introduce in two passes --- collapsing
antiparallel pairs, then deleting back-edges against a depth ordering --- so that
$H$ is a DAG by construction (C1).

\paragraph{Block-sum adjacency.}
For each ordered pair of distinct supernodes $(C, C')$, the signed \emph{block sum}
\begin{equation}
B_{C \to C'} \;=\; \sum_{u \in C,\, v \in C'} W_{vu}\,\mathbf{1}[(u, v) \in E']
\label{eq:block-sum}
\end{equation}
collects the signed weight of all pruned edges from members of $C$ to members of
$C'$ ($W_{vu}$ is the entry for the edge $u \to v$, target $v$ indexing the row,
source $u$ the column). The diagonal blocks ($C = C'$) are dropped: edges internal
to a supernode are absorbed into the node and contribute the loss
$L_{\text{causal}}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Lcausal}. The off-diagonal block sums form the
\emph{raw} supernode adjacency, which may still contain antiparallel pairs and
longer cycles.

\paragraph{Antiparallel collapse.}
For each unordered pair $\{C, C'\}$, let $a = B_{C \to C'}$ and $b = B_{C' \to C}$.
We keep only the \emph{net} flow along the dominant direction,
\begin{equation}
(B_{C \to C'},\, B_{C' \to C}) \;\longleftarrow\;
\begin{cases}
\bigl((|a| - |b|)\,\mathrm{sgn}(a),\; 0\bigr) & \text{if } |a| > |b|, \\[2pt]
\bigl(0,\; (|b| - |a|)\,\mathrm{sgn}(b)\bigr) & \text{if } |b| > |a|, \\[2pt]
(0,\; 0)                                       & \text{if } |a| = |b|,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:antiparallel}
\end{equation}
so every $2$-cycle is removed and the surviving edge carries the difference of the
two opposing magnitudes; mutually cancelling flow does not appear in $H$.

\paragraph{Back-edge removal.}
The collapsed adjacency can still contain longer cycles. We linearize the
supernodes by depth: an embedding supernode gets depth $-\infty$ (forced source),
a logit supernode $+\infty$ (forced sink), and a feature supernode the median
layer of its members (ties broken by mean member layer, then smallest member
$\text{ctx\_idx}$, then supernode index). Ranking the supernodes by this order, we
delete every edge $C \to C'$ whose source ranks after its target. The result is the
DAG adjacency $W_H$, with $W_{H,\, C \to C'} = B_{C \to C'}$ for the surviving
edges and $E_H = \{(C, C') : W_{H,\, C \to C'} \ne 0\}$.

\paragraph{Labeling.}
Finally, each supernode is given a short human-readable name and a coarse role
(input, abstract, or output) summarizing its members' feature descriptions. The
labels are a display overlay for the reader; they do not enter the structure of
$H$ or any quantity above.

\section{Chapter Summary}
This chapter formalized the summarization task and presented the three-stage method
that solves it. The summary graph $H$ was defined as a labeled DAG of supernodes
together with the partition map from the pruned attribution graph, and four
desiderata --- importance retention, atomicity, causal-information preservation, and
interpretability --- were turned into the pruning loss $L_{\text{prune}}$, the
atomicity loss $L_{\text{atom}}$, and the causal-preservation loss $L_{\text{causal}}$
under the acyclicity and budget constraints. Pruning meets D1 by importance-thresholded
selection, which also explains why every error node is removed. Clustering meets D2 and
trades it against D3 through the weighted objective $L_{\text{atom}} + \lambda
L_{\text{causal}}$, comparing features by a role similarity for which four variants were
given: the signed cosine consumed by the exact ILP and the three nonnegative
multi-kernel means consumed by the scalable spectral and agglomerative clusterers.
Interpreting meets D4 by aggregating the pruned edges into a block-sum adjacency and
removing cycles, yielding the DAG $H$. The next chapter analyzes the method's
theoretical properties, and Chapter~5 validates the resulting summary graphs
empirically.
